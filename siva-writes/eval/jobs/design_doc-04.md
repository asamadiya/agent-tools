Raw material. Aurora DSQL's stated goal: a relational database that removes scale, reliability, durability, and multi-region fault tolerance from what an application builder has to think about. A database of first resort, simple to adopt at low scale, growing with the application without adding complexity. The operational-simplicity argument applies to human-built systems and is magnified for agent-built ones, because agents build well but are weak at long-horizon tasks such as database operations. The architecture is disaggregated: multiple independent services, each owning a small number of well-defined concerns. DSQL is not the first disaggregated OLTP database; Aurora and DynamoDB came earlier. The lessons carried over from operating those at scale: avoid large caches, which make failovers and read scaling awkward; offer strongly consistent scalable reads, which removes whole classes of hot-key and partition problems; push expensive operations such as scans down into storage, where they run next to local SSD. DSQL goes further than predecessors by fully disaggregating replication from transaction isolation and from storage. Multi-tenant packing reduces the peak-to-average load ratio on a physical machine at approximately the rate of sqrt(number of loads); cost scales with peak while revenue scales with average.
