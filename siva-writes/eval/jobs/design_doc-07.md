Raw material. Framing for coding-agent capability, from an electrical-engineering analogy. Coding agents now build meaningful, correct software quickly and at very low cost, with super-human ability on some coding tasks. Computers have had super-human abilities for at least 85 years, dating to work such as Marian Rejewski's in the 1930s; a current MacBook adds 64-bit numbers roughly 100,000,000,000 times faster than a human. The pattern across those nine decades: vastly super-human in some areas, well short of human in others. The circuit: an opamp plus an analog multiplier IC, for example the long-obsolete MC1495. Apply a voltage on the left and the output on the right is its square root. Mechanism: the opamp drives its two inputs to equality, so feeding the output multiplied by itself back into one input forces the output to the square root of the input. Generalisation: components inside feedback loops can behave very differently from their open-loop behaviour, and excellent outputs can be extracted from poor components. AI agents are feedback loops built around an LLM, a component with useful but flawed open-loop behaviour. Developer tooling moved over roughly two years from open-loop autocomplete in IDEs to agents, relocating the build-test-iterate loop from the human into the agent. Write a design doc for a developer-tools team setting out how to reason about agent capability.
