Raw material, Cloudflare, 21 August 2025, all times UTC. A single customer began requesting large volumes of cached objects from AWS us-east-1 at about 16:27, and the response traffic doubled total Cloudflare-to-AWS traffic, saturating every direct peering link between the two networks. At 16:37 AWS started withdrawing BGP advertisements on congested private network interconnects to shed load; that steered traffic onto peering links reached through an offsite interconnection switch, which then also saturated. Two pre-existing conditions made it worse: one direct peering link was running at half capacity due to an earlier failure, and the Data Center Interconnect to the offsite switch was already due for a capacity upgrade. 16:44 the network team is alerted to congestion in Ashburn (IAD); 16:45 responders find that uncongested paths no longer carry AWS prefixes because of the withdrawals; 17:22 dropped traffic increases; 17:45 a customer-impact incident is raised; 19:05 rate-limiting the customer reduces congestion; 19:27 traffic engineering fully resolves congestion; 19:45 AWS begins reverting withdrawals; 20:07 AWS finishes normalizing announcements over IAD PNIs; 20:18 impact ends. Router queues grew and edge routers dropped high-priority packets throughout. Customers saw latency, timeouts and low throughput; the share of synthetic origin requests missing the latency SLO tracked the packet drops. Latency recovered after drops because both sides were renormalizing prefix advertisements. Regional only; global Cloudflare services were unaffected. Not an attack, not a BGP hijack.
