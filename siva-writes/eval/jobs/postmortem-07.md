Raw material, Cloudflare, 14 July 2025, all times UTC. The 1.1.1.1 public DNS Resolver was globally unavailable from 21:52 to 22:54, 62 minutes, with intermittent degradation of Gateway DNS. Origin: on 6 June at 17:38, a release preparing a service topology for a future Data Localization Suite service accidentally attached the 1.1.1.1 Resolver prefixes to that non-production service. Nothing changed on the production network, no traffic shifted, no alerts fired, and the error sat dormant. On 14 July at 21:48 an offline test location was attached to the same non-production service for internal testing. That triggered a global refresh of network configuration, which collapsed the 1.1.1.1 topology from all locations to one offline location and withdrew every Resolver prefix worldwide. 22:01 internal health alerts fire and the incident is declared; 22:20 revert initiated, prefixes re-advertised, traffic returns to about 77% of pre-incident levels; the remaining gap is roughly 23% of edge servers whose IP bindings had been removed and had to be reconfigured through change management, normally staged over hours, accelerated after validation in test locations; 22:54 normal traffic; 22:55 resolved. Affected prefixes include 1.1.1.0/24, 1.0.0.0/24, 2606:4700:4700::/48 and others. UDP, TCP and DoT query volume collapsed; DoH stayed mostly stable because most DoH users reach cloudflare-dns.com, which uses different addresses. At 21:54 Tata Communications India (AS4755) began advertising 1.1.1.0/24, a hijack exposed by the withdrawal but not a cause. The legacy topology system hard-codes location lists and does not use canary deployment despite peer review. Write the public postmortem for resolver users and network engineers.
