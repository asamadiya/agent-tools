Measuring availability the way customers experience it

Problem

Two arguments keep recurring, and in both of them everyone is right. A service reports a mean request time of 100ms; a user reports she waits a second. A service reports MTTR under a minute; a user reports that her outages last an hour. Nobody is lying and nobody is measuring wrong. The two sides are measuring different things.

We count events. One request is one sample; one outage is one sample. The customer does not sample events, she samples elapsed time — and the number of customers who land inside a given outage is proportional to how long that outage lasts. A five-second blip catches almost nobody. A six-hour recovery catches everybody, repeatedly. Our histogram gives both of them one row.

This is the inspection paradox. Customers see a length-weighted version of our distribution, not the distribution itself. If our mean is E[X], theirs is

  E_a[X] = E[X^2] / (2 E[X]) = 0.5 * (E[X] + Var(X)/E[X])

The gap is Var(X)/E[X], and for recovery times the variance is enormous relative to the mean. Fit a log-normal to plausible numbers: median TTR 30 minutes, p99 600 minutes — one event in a hundred taking ten hours. MTTR lands just over an hour. The customer-experienced mean lands at several hours. That is a multiple of the reported number, not a correction to it. Our dashboard is not slightly optimistic; it is reporting a different quantity.

Proposal

1. Report a time-weighted recovery metric alongside MTTR, computed directly as sum(d_i^2) / (2 * sum(d_i)) over incident durations in the window. Call it customer-experienced recovery time. Do not replace MTTR — it remains the right metric for asking how our response process is doing. It is the wrong metric for asking what customers lived through.

2. Publish the tail explicitly. p99 duration and the share of total downtime minutes contributed by the top 1% of incidents. When one incident owns half the downtime, the mean of the others is decoration.

3. Set SLOs on downtime-minutes and on the time-weighted mean, not on incident count. Counting incidents rewards closing the short ones fast.

4. Ship the simulator as the shared reference: log-normal service with mu=1.0, sigma=3.0, Poisson arrivals at rate 100, 1000 samples, reporting operator-side and customer-side means side by side. It settles these arguments in thirty seconds.

Non-goals

Timeout-and-retry masks some service-time tail and is already deployed where it helps. It does nothing for recovery time — there is no retry around an outage. Latency and availability need separate treatment here.
