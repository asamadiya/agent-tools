search-federator canary 2026.9.3: SLO breach on canary hosts, revert decided Sep 15
Owner: Dan Ortiz. Status: canary held, re-canary scheduled Sep 22.

The canary build breaches the service p99 SLO. Since Sep 8 the build has run on 4 of 240 production
hosts, carrying 1.7% of traffic at 2,900 QPS per host. p99 response time on those hosts is 311 ms
against 184 ms on the control fleet, an increase of 127 ms or 69%. The SLO is p99 under 250 ms, so
the canary hosts sit 61 ms over the line. p50 moved from 41 ms to 42 ms, which tells us the added
cost lands in the tail and not on the typical request.

A new gRPC interceptor validates every response payload against a JSON schema on the serving path,
synchronously. Its median cost is 0.9 ms and its p99 cost is 94 ms, and the p99 figure is dominated
by schema recompilation on a cache miss. The schema cache is a 512-entry LRU. Production traffic
touches roughly 3,400 distinct schemas, so the cache holds about 15% of the working set. Measured
hit rate on canary is 71%, above the 15% a uniform access pattern would give, which says schema
access is heavily skewed toward a small hot set. The remaining 29% of requests recompile: about 841
recompilations per second per host.

The pre-release load test could not have caught this. That test exercised 300 schemas against a
512-entry cache, so after warmup nothing was ever evicted and the hit rate came in at 99.6%. The
test measured a warm cache, not an LRU under eviction pressure. Any schema count under 512 would
have produced the same clean result.

One number in the analysis is not yet fully accounted for. The interceptor's own p99 of 94 ms does
not explain all 127 ms of added tail latency, and the two percentiles do not add, so we cannot
subtract them to get a remainder. Requests queueing behind a recompiling thread is the likeliest
source of the gap. We have not instrumented that path and will not claim the number until we have.

The error budget is untouched. Canary carries 1.7% of traffic and the fleet-wide p99 is still set by
the 236 control hosts, so budget consumed since Sep 8 is zero. The exposure is real for the requests
that landed on those 4 hosts and invisible in the aggregate SLO.

Decision taken Sep 15, three changes:

1. Revert the interceptor from the release branch. 2026.9.3 ships without it.
2. Raise the schema cache from 512 to 8,192 entries, which is 2.4x the observed 3,400-schema working
   set and leaves room for growth before eviction pressure returns.
3. Put validation behind a sampling flag set to 1%. At 2,900 QPS that is 29 validated responses per
   second per host, and a cold cache at that rate warms in minutes rather than thrashing.

Re-canary is Sep 22 on the same 4 hosts. The gate is p99 under 250 ms and cache hit rate above 99%
at production schema cardinality, both held for 72 hours. Watch the hit rate first: if 8,192 entries
still miss more than 1% of the time, the working set is larger than the 3,400 we measured and the
cache size needs deriving from a fresh count instead of from this week's.

Ask for SLO owners: confirm the 72-hour hold is long enough to cover a weekday traffic peak, since
the Sep 8-15 window may not have included one.
