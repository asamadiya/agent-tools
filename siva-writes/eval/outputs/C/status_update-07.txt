Trim: monthly programme status

Owner: Renee Alvarez. Finance partner: Sanjay Kothari. Reporting cadence: monthly.

Trim has booked $16.4M of its $28M annualised infrastructure savings target, 59% of the
commitment, with the FY27 deadline still ahead of us. The three workstreams that produced
that number are all past their hardest execution phase. The $11.6M that remains is
concentrated in two workstreams, one of which we do not control.

Right-sizing contributed $9.1M, the largest single line. We cut requests on 6,800
over-provisioned pods and moved the median CPU request from 4.0 cores to 1.5, a 62%
reduction in requested capacity per pod. Storage deletion contributed $4.2M by removing
41 PB of object storage that no client had touched in more than three years. Reserved
instance conversion contributed $3.1M and now covers 62% of the steady-state fleet.

Right-sizing also caused five incidents: four SEV-3 and one SEV-2. Every one of them was
CPU throttling on a service whose p99 CPU already sat close to the new request, which
means we were cutting into live headroom and not into slack. We shipped a guardrail on
Aug 20 that blocks any cut where measured p99 CPU exceeds 70% of the proposed request.
Since then the guardrail has rejected 410 of 1,100 candidate cuts, 37%. No right-sizing
incident has occurred since Aug 20.

Read that 37% rejection rate as a correction to our earlier sizing model, not as friction.
Before the guardrail we would have shipped those 410 cuts, and the incident record says
roughly what fraction of them would have paged someone. The tradeoff is that the
right-sizing pipeline now yields less per candidate than the $9.1M run rate implies, so
forecasts that extrapolate from the first 6,800 pods will run high.

GPU bin-packing carries $7.0M of the remaining $11.6M and is the programme's single
largest risk. Cluster utilization is 38% today against a 65% target, a 27-point gap. The
work depends on a scheduler change owned by another org that has no committed date, which
means we currently hold no schedule for 60% of the unbooked savings. Pull that date in or
the $28M target moves. We are treating this as the one item that needs a decision at this
review: either the scheduler change gets a date and an owner on record, or we rebaseline
the FY27 number to $21M and say so now.

Decommissioning three legacy datacenter halls carries the other $4.6M and is scheduled to
complete by June 2027. That date leaves no buffer inside FY27, and the savings do not
begin to accrue until the halls are actually dark, so a one-quarter slip pushes recognition
out of the fiscal year entirely. We will bring a hall-by-hall migration schedule to the
October review with the workload count still resident in each.

Two things we would like from this forum. First, an escalation path to the scheduler
owning org, ideally at the VP level, since our own requests have not produced a date in two
months. Second, agreement on how we report against $28M while the GPU line has no
schedule: we would prefer to show booked savings plus a scheduled-and-unscheduled split
each month instead of a single percentage that implies uniform confidence across the
remainder.

Forecast for the next 90 days: right-sizing adds $0.8M to $1.2M at the post-guardrail
yield, reserved instance conversion adds roughly $0.4M as we move coverage from 62% toward
75%, and storage deletion is largely exhausted after the 41 PB sweep. That puts us near
$18M booked by the December review, 64% of target, with the GPU dependency unchanged
unless the escalation lands.
