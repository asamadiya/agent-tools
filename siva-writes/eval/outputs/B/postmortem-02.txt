Impact analysis: 18 November 2025 incident (all times UTC)

Workers KV returned heavily elevated 5xx for the length of the incident, and every service downstream of it inherited that failure. The KV front-end gateway calls our core proxy, and the core proxy was failing, so the gateway had no path to serve reads or writes. At 13:04 we deployed a patch that let Workers KV bypass the core proxy entirely. Error rates fell at that point for KV itself and for everything reading from it, including Access and the Dashboard.

Cloudflare Access failed authentication requests widely from the start of the incident until the rollback at 13:05. Existing sessions were unaffected: a user who had already authenticated kept reaching your applications throughout. Every failed authentication terminated at a Cloudflare error page, which is the point that matters for your security review -- no user reached a protected application without authenticating, and the logins that did succeed were recorded correctly. Access configuration changes you attempted during the window either failed outright or propagated very slowly. All of them have since been recovered, and you do not need to reapply them.

The Cloudflare Dashboard was unavailable across two separate windows, 11:30 to 13:10 and 14:40 to 15:30, and the two have different causes. The Dashboard reads Workers KV internally and calls Turnstile in its login flow. Turnstile failed to load through the first window, so an administrator without an active session could not log in at all. The KV bypass ended that window at 13:10. The second window opened after we restored the feature file: queued login attempts and client retries arrived together, and the control plane could not absorb the backlog. We scaled control plane concurrency at roughly 15:30 and the Dashboard recovered.

Email Security continued to process and deliver mail throughout, with degraded detection. The service lost access to one IP reputation source, which reduced spam-detection accuracy and suppressed some detections that key on domain age. No mail was dropped or delayed. A number of Auto Move actions failed to apply; we have identified the affected messages and remediated them.

CDN response latency rose for the duration of the incident, through a path independent of the products above. Our debugging and observability systems enrich uncaught errors before recording them, the volume of uncaught errors ran far above normal, and that enrichment consumed large amounts of CPU on the machines serving your traffic. Requests were answered; they were answered more slowly.

Three checks are worth running on your side. Pull your Access authentication logs for 11:30 to 13:05 and reconcile the successful logins against your expectation -- those records are accurate, so a gap in them is a genuine failed attempt rather than lost telemetry. Review any Access configuration change you made during that window and confirm the current state matches what you intended, since a slow-propagating change may have landed later than you observed it. For Email Security, re-examine quarantine and inbox placement for mail that arrived between 11:30 and 15:30; what you lost there was accuracy, and messages that should have been quarantined may be sitting in user inboxes.

The Access, Dashboard and Email Security effects all trace back to the same core proxy failure, and the 13:04 KV bypass is the single change that cleared the bulk of them. The Dashboard's second window and the Email Security remediation are the two pieces of recovery that ran past it.
