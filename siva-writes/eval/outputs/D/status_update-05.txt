two-tower-v7 wrap-up: the session head helped heavy users and flattened ranking for everyone else

We are not shipping two-tower-v7, because the session-aware retrieval head raised notification CTR 1.9% for the top decile of members by weekly session count and cost the 62% of members with fewer than three sessions a week 0.8%, and that larger segment holds aggregate CTR at -0.11%, 95% CI -0.41% to +0.19%.

The regression that decided the call was downstream, not CTR. Sessions per member fell 0.6% at p = 0.003. The LIX ramped to 2% on Jul 14, 4% on Aug 4 and 10% on Aug 19, and every number here comes from the 10% arm between Aug 19 and Sep 5. A CTR delta of -0.11% that cannot be separated from zero, paired with a 0.6% session loss that can, says the head changed which notifications members received without making them better ones, and the members who returned less often are what we paid for the change.

The segment cut is where the aggregate becomes legible. Heavy members gained 1.9% CTR; light members lost 0.8%; light members are 62% of the population. Weighting those two effects by segment size lands the aggregate inside the noise band we measured, so the flat headline is not a weak effect. It is two opposed effects of comparable magnitude, one of which lands on most of our members.

Our working hypothesis is that the head needs roughly four prior sessions before its embedding carries member-specific signal. Below that it falls back to a near-constant vector, which enters the score as close to an additive constant across candidates. A constant shifts every candidate by the same amount and preserves the ordering the other towers produced only where their margins exceed the noise the fallback adds, so light users receive a ranking pulled toward the population prior. Test it directly: bucket members by prior session count at scoring time and measure the per-bucket variance of the session embedding. The variance should sit near zero below four sessions and rise above it.

Serving cost rose 31% for that outcome. The treatment consumed 4,100 GPU-hours per day against 3,130 for control, and p99 scoring latency moved from 38 ms to 57 ms. The 19 ms of added tail latency lands in a notifications path with little headroom above its current budget. Even the optimistic end of the CTR interval, +0.19%, would not have covered either number, and the pessimistic end, -0.41%, would have bought a CTR loss for 970 extra GPU-hours a day.

The metrics council decided on Sep 5 to tear down the LIX and not ramp further. The session embedding table stays in the feature store, because two other teams now read it and dropping it would break them for no gain; the recurring cost we are removing is the serving-time head, not the table. Teardown of the scoring path is what returns the 970 GPU-hours per day and the 19 ms of tail.

A second attempt should gate the head on session count instead of falling back to a constant vector. Route members below the threshold to the v6 scorer and run the session head only above it, which confines the head to the population that actually gained. Two caveats bind that plan. The 62% figure counts members with fewer than three sessions a week, and the hypothesised threshold counts four prior sessions at scoring time; those are different populations, and sizing the gated arm needs a prior-session-count histogram we have not built. The gate also has to beat control on sessions per member, not only on CTR, since sessions per member is the metric that failed at p = 0.003 and CTR never separated from zero at all.

Aditi Sundaram owns the follow-up. The histogram and the per-bucket variance measurement are the two things worth doing before anyone writes model code.
