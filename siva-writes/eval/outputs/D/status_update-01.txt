Pylon to Helix migration: Dec 12 holds on arithmetic, the exemptions are what break it

The Dec 12 completion date survives the current burn rate with four weeks of slack, and the only thing on the board that can break it is two teams asking for exemptions that outlive the hardware they depend on. As of Sep 18 we have cut over 744 of 1,240 services, 60%, since starting Apr 6. Remaining scope is 496 services, 118 of them tier-0.

The schedule math is not close. Trailing six-week burn is 62 services per week, so 496 remaining services is 8.0 weeks of work. Sep 18 to Dec 12 is 85 days, 12.1 weeks. That leaves 4.1 weeks of margin, which is roughly 250 services of absorbed slippage before the date is at risk. The margin is real but it is not distributed evenly, because 96 of the 496 cannot start yet.

Those 96 services call watchAndReload, an API Helix does not implement. The compatibility shim is in code review with an ETA of Oct 9. If it lands on that date, those 96 services have 9.1 weeks to migrate against a 1.5-week nominal burn, and the slack absorbs a slip of up to six weeks in the shim itself. If the shim slips past Nov 21 the 96 services cannot finish and Dec 12 fails. That is the single dependency worth watching weekly, and it is a code review, not an engineering unknown.

Payments and Trust have each requested exemptions through Q1 and neither has filed a migration plan. Q1 2027 ends Mar 31. The Pylon hardware lease expires Feb 28 2027 and will not be renewed. An exemption granted as written therefore extends 31 days past the point at which Pylon has no hardware to run on, which means granting it commits us to either a lease renewal we have already declined or an emergency migration of two tier-0-adjacent teams in February. Running both stores in parallel costs $310K per month, so every month of exemption past Dec 12 also costs $310K that is currently budgeted to stop.

The rollback record argues for holding the current rate. Three rollbacks in 23 weeks, all completed inside 24 hours. The worst was notify-dispatch on Aug 27, a 9-minute partial outage in which 12% of push notifications were delayed. That is the observed cost of the failure mode at 62 services per week across 744 cutovers, and it argues against adding process that would drop the rate, since a week of slowdown costs 62 services of margin against a blast radius measured in minutes.

The 118 remaining tier-0 services are the part of the plan with the least evidence behind it. All 744 completed cutovers include tier-0 work, and the three rollbacks came from that population, but we have not yet sequenced the remaining 118 against the shim date. We will publish that sequence by Oct 2 so the tier-0 tail does not land in the last two weeks of the quarter.

Three decisions from this QBR. First, deny the Payments and Trust exemptions as filed and require both teams to submit a migration plan with a completion date on or before Dec 12 within two weeks of this meeting; an exemption that ends after Feb 28 2027 is a lease decision, not a scheduling one, and it belongs to the VP. Second, treat the Oct 9 shim date as a reviewable commitment and escalate at the weekly sync if the review has not merged by Oct 2. Third, hold the burn rate at 62 per week and do not add pre-cutover gates; the three rollbacks cost 9 minutes of degraded push delivery in total, and the parallel-run clock costs $310K per month.
