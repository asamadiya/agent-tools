Impact analysis: downstream services, 18 November 2025 (all times UTC)

Every downstream failure in this incident arrived through one of two edges in the dependency graph -- Workers KV's front-end gateway calling the failing core proxy, or Turnstile in the Dashboard login flow -- and not through independent faults in the affected products. Workers KV returned heavily elevated 5xx for the duration because its gateway sits in front of the core proxy and inherited the proxy's failures. That is why one patch fixed most of the blast radius at once: at 13:04 we let Workers KV bypass the core proxy, and error rates fell across every service that reads KV, Access among them.

Cloudflare Access failed closed, so no user reached a target application on a broken authentication. Authentication failed widely from the start of the incident until the rollback at 13:05. Every failed authentication terminated at an error page, existing Access sessions continued to work throughout, and the logins that did succeed during the window were logged correctly. Your access logs for this window are complete and no unauthorized session was created by the failure mode.

Access configuration changes attempted during the window are the one place where you should verify state yourself. Updates submitted while the incident was active either failed outright or propagated very slowly; all have since been recovered. Re-read any Access policy or application configuration you pushed between the start of the incident and 13:05, and confirm it matches what you intended.

The Dashboard lost availability in two windows with different causes. The first ran 11:30 to 13:10 and ended when the Workers KV bypass reached the Dashboard's internal KV dependency at 13:10; through it, Turnstile failed to load, so anyone without an active session had no path through the login flow at all. The second ran 14:40 to 15:30 and was produced by our own recovery: once the feature file was restored, a backlog of queued login attempts plus client retries exceeded control plane capacity. Scaling control plane concurrency around 15:30 ended it.

Email Security delivered mail throughout and degraded in detection accuracy, not in delivery. The service lost access to an IP reputation source, which lowered spam-detection accuracy and suppressed some new-domain-age detections. Some Auto Move actions failed during the window and have since been remediated. Mail that arrived between 11:30 and 15:30 passed through a weaker filter than normal, so treat that window's delivered mail as under-filtered and re-scan it against your own quarantine policy.

CDN response latency rose from observability overhead rather than from capacity loss. The debugging and observability systems consumed large amounts of CPU enriching the uncaught errors the core proxy was generating, and that CPU came out of the same machines serving your traffic. Latency in this window is not a signal about your origin, your cache hit ratio, or your plan's capacity.

Three items need action on your side. Re-verify Access configuration pushed before 13:05. Re-scan email delivered during the incident window against your quarantine policy, because the IP reputation source was missing from the decision. Discount CDN latency between 11:30 and 15:30 when you compute the month's performance baselines, and tell us if you see elevated latency after 15:30, because that would be impact we have not accounted for here.
