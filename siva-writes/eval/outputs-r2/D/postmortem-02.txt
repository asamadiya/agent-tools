Impact analysis: downstream services (all times UTC)

Enterprise customers lost authentication and configuration control during this incident, not traffic serving, and every downstream failure traces to a single dependency: the Workers KV front-end gateway calls the core proxy, the core proxy was failing, and Workers KV therefore returned heavily elevated 5xx to everything reading from it. At 13:04 a patch let Workers KV bypass the core proxy. Error rates fell across every service downstream of KV within minutes of that patch, which is the strongest evidence we have that the dependency chain above is the whole explanation and not one contributing factor among several.

Cloudflare Access returned widespread authentication failures from the start of the incident until the rollback at 13:05. Existing Access sessions continued to work throughout, so the population affected is users who needed to authenticate during the window, not your full user base. Every failed authentication terminated at an error page. No user reached a target application while authentication was broken, and logins that did succeed during the window were logged correctly. Your Access logs for this period are complete, and there is no case where a request reached an application without passing policy.

Access configuration updates attempted during the window either failed outright or propagated very slowly. All have since been recovered. Check any policy or application change you submitted between the incident start and 13:05 against what is live now: a change that appeared to fail may have landed late, and a change that appeared to land may be the recovered copy, not your submission.

The Dashboard was unavailable across two separate windows, 11:30 to 13:10 and 14:40 to 15:30, and only the first was caused by the original fault. The Dashboard depends on Workers KV internally and on Turnstile in the login flow; Turnstile failed to load, which left anyone without an active session unable to log in at all. The KV bypass ended the first window at 13:10. The second window was produced by our own recovery: restoring the feature file released a backlog of queued login attempts plus their retries, which exceeded control plane capacity until we scaled control plane concurrency around 15:30.

Email Security kept processing and delivering mail for the full incident, and the degradation was in detection accuracy, not in delivery. The service lost access to an IP reputation source, which reduced spam-detection accuracy by an amount we have not quantified and suppressed some new-domain-age detections. Some Auto Move actions failed during the window and have since been remediated. Mail delivered to your users during this period received weaker filtering than normal. Treat the window as a period of elevated false-negative risk and review inbox placement for messages from domains registered recently.

CDN response latency rose for reasons unrelated to Workers KV. Debugging and observability systems consumed large amounts of CPU enriching uncaught errors, and that enrichment work competed with request serving on the same machines. The observability path amplified the incident rather than only reporting it.

Two actions follow for enterprise customers. Re-verify every Access configuration change attempted before 13:05, because the recovery restored state and did not replay your submissions. Audit Email Security dispositions for the incident window, with attention to new-domain senders and to mail that Auto Move should have relocated and did not.
