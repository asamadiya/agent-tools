Congestion between Cloudflare and AWS us-east-1, 21 August 2025

If your origin is in AWS us-east-1, between 16:27 and 20:18 UTC on 21 August 2025 your requests through Cloudflare saw added latency, timeouts and reduced throughput, because one customer's cache-fill traffic doubled total Cloudflare-to-AWS traffic and saturated the peering links that carry it. All times below are UTC. Impact was regional: Cloudflare's global services were unaffected, as were origins outside AWS us-east-1. This was not an attack and not a BGP hijack.

The trigger was a single customer that began requesting large volumes of cached objects from us-east-1 at about 16:27. The response traffic doubled the volume Cloudflare pulls from AWS, and every direct peering link between the two networks reached line rate. Peering capacity is fixed and provisioned against an observed demand curve; a 2x step in minutes leaves no headroom to absorb. Router queues filled, and edge routers dropped packets across all classes, including high-priority traffic. A saturated queue drops what arrives, and a priority class does not create bandwidth.

AWS began withdrawing BGP advertisements on the congested private network interconnects at 16:37 to shed load, and the withdrawal relocated the congestion instead of reducing it. The demand was unchanged, and the same bytes took the next-best path: peering links reached through an offsite interconnection switch, which then saturated as well. The withdrawals had a second effect that cost us time. When responders looked at 16:45 for uncongested paths to move traffic onto, those paths no longer carried AWS prefixes, and the spare capacity was unreachable for exactly the traffic that needed it.

Two pre-existing conditions narrowed the margin before any of this started. One direct peering link was running at half its normal capacity following an earlier failure, and the Data Center Interconnect to the offsite switch was already scheduled for a capacity upgrade. Neither would have mattered at normal load. Both removed headroom that would otherwise have absorbed part of the doubled traffic.

What you would have observed tracks the packet drops rather than the BGP state. Our synthetic origin-request monitoring shows the share of requests missing the latency SLO rising and falling with the drop rate, which is the signal to correlate against if you are reconciling your own graphs. Latency stayed elevated after drops had subsided, because both networks were still renormalizing prefix advertisements and traffic was riding suboptimal paths during that window.

Timeline. 16:27 the customer's fetches begin. 16:37 AWS starts withdrawing advertisements on congested PNIs. 16:44 our network team is alerted to congestion in Ashburn (IAD), 17 minutes after onset. 16:45 responders find uncongested paths no longer carry AWS prefixes. 17:22 dropped traffic increases. 17:45 a customer-impact incident is raised, 78 minutes after onset. 19:05 rate-limiting the customer reduces congestion. 19:27 traffic engineering fully resolves congestion. 19:45 AWS begins reverting the withdrawals and finishes over IAD PNIs at 20:07. 20:18 impact ends. Total duration 3 hours 51 minutes.

The fix that worked was rate-limiting the origin fetches at source at 19:05, and it worked because it was the only action taken that reduced demand; every earlier action moved demand. That 2 hours 38 minutes from onset to the first demand-side action is the number we are working to cut.

Four things change as a result. We are adding per-customer rate limits on origin fetches that trigger on the rate of change in egress toward a single origin network, not on an absolute threshold, since the absolute volume here was servable and the slope was not. We are restoring the degraded peering link to full capacity and pulling the IAD Data Center Interconnect upgrade forward. We are adding alerting on peering-link utilisation that fires before queues build, closing the 17-minute detection gap. We are working with AWS on a load-shedding response that does not withdraw prefixes from paths our operators need to reach.

We are sorry for the disruption. If you saw impact outside this window or outside us-east-1, contact Cloudflare Support so we can reconcile it against our record.
