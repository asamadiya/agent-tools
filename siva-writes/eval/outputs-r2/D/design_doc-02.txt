Measuring availability and recovery as the customer samples it

Availability and recovery targets should be set on the length-weighted mean of recovery time, E[X^2] / (2 E[X]), because that is the quantity a customer actually samples, and on real recovery distributions it runs several times larger than the MTTR we publish.

Two familiar disputes are both sides being right about different averages. A service reports a mean request time of 100 ms; a user reports her mean wait is 1 s. A service reports MTTR under 1 minute; a user reports a mean outage length of 1 hour. The operator counts events, weighting one request or one outage equally with every other. The customer samples elapsed time, and the number of customers who land inside a given outage is proportional to that outage's length.

That length weighting is the entire gap, and it has a closed form. Customers do not see f(t); they see a t-weighted version of it, whose mean is E_a[X] = E[X^2] / (2 E[X]) = 0.5 * (E[X] + Var(X) / E[X]). The excess over half the operator mean is Var(X) / E[X], and recovery times carry variance that is very large relative to their mean, because a handful of long recoveries dominate the second moment while contributing almost nothing to the event count.

The multiplier is not a rounding correction. Fit a log-normal to a plausible recovery profile: median TTR 30 minutes, p99 600 minutes (10 hours, one event in 100). That fit yields an MTTR just over 1 hour and a customer-experienced mean of several hours. Reproduce it before believing it: draw failure durations from a log-normal with mu = 1.0 and sigma = 3.0, arrive clients as a Poisson process at rate 100, take 1000 samples, and compare the mean duration against the mean duration observed by an arriving client.

Report three recovery numbers instead of one, and put the target on the third. Publish median TTR, p99 TTR, and the length-weighted mean E[X^2] / (2 E[X]) computed from the same incident set. The median tells the on-call engineer what a typical repair costs. The p99 and the weighted mean tell the platform what customers spent waiting, and a p99 of 600 minutes against a median of 30 says the recovery work worth funding is the tail, not the middle.

Engineering the tail down is the only lever on the customer number. A client-side timeout with retry can hide part of a service-time tail, and nothing at any layer hides a recovery-time tail: the customer is inside the outage for its full remaining length. Cutting p99 TTR from 600 minutes to 120 minutes moves the customer-experienced mean far more than cutting the median from 30 minutes to 20.
