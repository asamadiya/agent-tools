Trim monthly programme status
Owner: Renee Alvarez. Finance partner: Sanjay Kothari.

Trim has booked $16.4M of the $28M annualised infrastructure savings target, 59%, against a
close date at the end of FY27. The programme is on plan by dollars booked and off plan by
risk concentration: 60% of what remains sits behind a dependency we do not control.

Three workstreams produced the $16.4M. Right-sizing 6,800 over-provisioned pods returned
$9.1M and moved the median CPU request from 4.0 cores to 1.5. Deleting 41 PB of object
storage untouched for more than three years returned $4.2M. Converting 62% of the
steady-state fleet to reserved instances returned $3.1M. These are annualised run-rate
figures validated with finance, not cash already recognised in FY26.

Right-sizing cost five incidents: four SEV-3 and one SEV-2. Every one was CPU throttling on a
service whose p99 CPU sat close to its new request, so the failure mode is a single sizing
error repeated, not five separate causes. A guardrail shipped Aug 20 now blocks any cut where
measured p99 CPU exceeds 70% of the proposed request. It has rejected 410 of 1,100 candidate
cuts since, 37%.

That 37% is the number I would look at hardest this month. It is the sizing model's error rate
measured against real telemetry, and it says better than a third of the remaining pod
population was scoped by a method that would have throttled it. The $9.1M already booked was
produced by the same method, so a portion of it is likely sitting on headroom that services
will reclaim. We are re-running the booked cohort against the guardrail threshold and will
report the exposure in the October review.

The remaining $11.6M breaks into $7.0M from GPU bin-packing and $4.6M from decommissioning
three legacy datacenter halls by June 2027. GPU cluster utilization is 38% today against a 65%
target, and the gap is real capacity rather than an accounting artifact. Capturing it requires
a scheduler change owned by another org that carries no committed date.

If that scheduler change does not land in time to bin-pack and hold utilization for a full
annualised period, Trim closes FY27 at $21.0M, 75% of target, with a $7.0M miss. I am
reporting that as the planning case, not the downside case, because a dependency with no date
is not a plan. We have no substitute workstream sized anywhere near $7.0M.

The $4.6M hall decommission is the healthier half of the remainder, and its June 2027
completion date leaves roughly one quarter of slack before FY27 close. Slip beyond September
2027 removes the annualised booking entirely, so the schedule matters more than the dollar
figure suggests. Migration planning for the workloads still resident in those halls is the
gating item, and it is on track.

What I need from this review is an escalation path to a committed scheduler date. Renee and I
have working-level agreement with the owning org and no roadmap commitment, which is the
normal outcome when the requesting team holds the savings target and the delivering team holds
none of it. The specific ask is that the scheduler change be given a dated commitment for a
quarter no later than Q2 FY27, or that we agree now to re-baseline Trim to $21.0M and redirect
the effort currently sized against GPU bin-packing.

Next month I will bring three things: the guardrail-adjusted exposure on the booked $9.1M, a
dated response on the scheduler dependency, and the hall migration plan with workload counts.
If the scheduler answer is still open in October, I will propose the re-baseline rather than
carry an undated $7.0M forward into a third consecutive report.
