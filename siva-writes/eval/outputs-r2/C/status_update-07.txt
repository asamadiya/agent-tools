Trim -- monthly programme status, September 2026
Owner: Renee Alvarez. Finance partner: Sanjay Kothari.

Booked savings stand at $16.4M annualised against the $28M FY27 target, 59% of
the commitment. Three workstreams produced all of it. Right-sizing 6,800
over-provisioned pods booked $9.1M and moved the median CPU request from 4.0
cores to 1.5. Deleting 41 PB of object storage untouched for more than three
years booked $4.2M. Converting 62% of the steady-state fleet to reserved
instances booked $3.1M. None of the three needs further engineering investment
to hold its run rate.

The remaining $11.6M sits in two plans that carry very different risk.
Decommissioning three legacy datacenter halls accounts for $4.6M, has a June
2027 completion date, and is tracking. GPU bin-packing accounts for the other
$7.0M and has no date at all. Cluster utilization is 38% today against the 65%
target the $7.0M was priced at, and closing that gap requires a scheduler
change owned by another org that has not committed a delivery date. One
external dependency therefore gates 25% of the full programme target and 60% of
everything still unbooked.

Right-sizing caused five incidents, four SEV-3 and one SEV-2. All five were CPU
throttling on services whose p99 CPU already sat close to the new request, so
the cut removed the headroom those services used at peak. A guardrail shipped
August 20 now blocks any cut where measured p99 CPU exceeds 70% of the proposed
request. It has rejected 410 of the 1,100 candidate cuts evaluated since, 37%.
No right-sizing incident has occurred after August 20.

Read that 37% rejection rate carefully before treating right-sizing as a
finished workstream. Roughly one candidate cut in three was unsafe under the
current threshold, which means the $9.1M booked is the safe fraction of the
original pod population and not the full opportunity we scoped. We are holding
the 70% threshold through Q4 and will not relax it to recover rejected
candidates. Re-examine the 410 rejections after the services behind them have
had their own headroom work, which is a separate programme and is not counted
in Trim.

Forecast. If the scheduler change lands with enough runway to convert
utilization from 38% to 65% before the fiscal year closes, Trim reaches $28M.
If it does not, the programme books $21.0M, 75% of target, since $16.4M plus
the $4.6M hall decommission is the total we can deliver without it. That is the
number to plan against today, because no committed date exists to plan against
instead. The $7.0M is unforecastable at present, and calling it late would
imply a schedule we have never been given.

Ask for this review. We need the scheduler org to commit to a date, or to state
that the change will not land in FY27. Either answer is usable; silence is not.
Renee will take the escalation if this review gives it a named executive sponsor
on the other side. A decision by the October review keeps a full-year booking
plausible.

Two things to watch between now and October. The reserved-instance conversion
covers 62% of the steady-state fleet, and the marginal savings on the next
tranche drop as the fleet shrinks under right-sizing, so we will requote that
$3.1M against the post-right-sizing footprint before extending coverage. The
storage deletion booked $4.2M against a one-time sweep of 41 PB; recurrence
depends on retention policy changes that are not yet written, and no further
storage savings are in the $11.6M plan.

Net position: 59% booked, one workstream fully blocked on an external
dependency, and a guardrail that has held for 30 days across 410 rejected cuts.
