1.1.1.1 Resolver unavailable for 62 minutes on 14 July 2025

All times UTC.

The 1.1.1.1 public DNS Resolver was unavailable worldwide from 21:52 to 22:54 on 14 July 2025, 62 minutes, and Gateway DNS was intermittently degraded over the same window. We withdrew the Resolver's own anycast prefixes from every Cloudflare location. This was our error, not an attack and not a BGP hijack, and we are sorry for the DNS failures this caused on your networks.

The cause was planted five weeks earlier. On 6 June at 17:38 we released a service topology for a future Data Localization Suite service. That release attached the 1.1.1.1 Resolver prefixes to a non-production service by mistake. Nothing moved: the production network kept the correct configuration, no traffic shifted, and no alert fired, because the non-production service had no locations attached and therefore produced no change to advertise. A misconfiguration that produces no diff is invisible to every check we had.

On 14 July at 21:48 an engineer attached a single offline test location to that same non-production service for internal testing. Attaching a location triggers a global refresh of network configuration. The refresh recomputed the topology for every prefix bound to the service, including the Resolver prefixes bound there on 6 June, and collapsed 1.1.1.1 from the full list of Cloudflare locations to the one offline test location. Within four minutes the edge withdrew 1.1.1.0/24, 1.0.0.0/24, 2606:4700:4700::/48 and the other Resolver prefixes from the global routing table. With no route to the anycast addresses, queries had nowhere to land.

UDP, TCP and DoT query volume to 1.1.1.1 collapsed to near zero. DNS-over-HTTPS held up: most DoH clients query cloudflare-dns.com, which resolves to different addresses that were never withdrawn. If your resolver configuration points at the literal 1.1.1.1 address over any transport, you saw the full outage.

Internal health alerts fired at 22:01 and we declared an incident. We identified the configuration change and began the revert at 22:20. Re-advertising the prefixes returned traffic to roughly 77% of pre-incident levels almost immediately. The missing 23% came from edge servers whose IP address bindings the refresh had torn down. Restoring those bindings is a change-management operation that we normally stage over hours. We validated the restore in test locations first, then accelerated the rollout across the fleet. Traffic reached normal levels at 22:54 and we marked the incident resolved at 22:55.

At 21:54, while 1.1.1.0/24 was unrouted by us, Tata Communications India (AS4755) began advertising the prefix. We do not believe this was related to our change, and it did not cause or lengthen the outage. A withdrawn prefix is an open invitation, and this is what the invitation looks like: a route that was always wrong but previously lost the best-path comparison became the only route on offer. RPKI-validating networks rejected it. Networks that do not validate may have sent Resolver queries to AS4755 for part of the window.

Two properties of the legacy topology system turned a scoped mistake into a global one. Location lists are hard-coded in the service definition, so a topology can name the complete fleet or a single machine with no structural difference between the two and no notion of a blast radius attached to the difference. The system also has no canary stage. A topology change either applies everywhere or nowhere, which means the only place a bad topology is ever exercised is production. Peer review covered the 6 June change and did not catch the wrong prefix binding, which is what we should expect from review as the sole control over a config path: reviewers compare intent to text, and here the text was plausible.

We have deprecated the legacy topology system for new service definitions and are migrating existing ones to the system that already gates changes through staged, health-checked deployment. Until that migration finishes, topology changes touching production prefixes go out progressively, with abort on health signal, and prefix withdrawals of this scale page before they propagate rather than after.
